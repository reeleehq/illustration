illustration.frames#

Text-to-frame search over a video corpus: find where in a set of videos something is.

“A cartoon wall clock”, “a bedroom at night”, “Tokyo Tower”: instead of scrubbing, index the videos’ frames once with SigLIP, then ask in words. One more illustration source, where an “image” is (video, t) (#46).

>>> from illustration.frames import index_video_frames, search_frames
>>> index_video_frames(["https://youtu.be/<id>", ...])
>>> for hit in search_frames("a cartoon wall clock", k=12):
...     print(hit.watch_url, round(hit.score, 3), hit.license)

Three seams, with working defaults.

  • sampler: a video URL to its frames. The default, storyboard_sampler(), reads YouTube’s storyboard (the seek-bar

    thumbnails, 320x180, one every few seconds) through yb.download.youtube_storyboard: no video download, and it works where video downloads hit a bot wall. Anything returning an object with video_id, url, title, duration, license and frames() ((t, PIL image) pairs) fits, e.g. frames sampled from a local file.

  • scorer: what embeds the frames and the query. The default is the package’s SigLIP 2 scorer (illustration.reranking.SiglipScorer, the [rerank] extra); anything with embed_text(text) and embed_pil_images(images, *, batch) fits (an ONNX text encoder on a machine without torch, for instance), and its model_id (if it has one) is recorded with the corpus: a search with another model skips those videos and warns, since their vectors are not comparable.

  • store: where the corpus lives, one entry per video (its times, its vectors, its title and licence). Default: JSON files under the cache dir, per model, since a corpus is regenerable.

Limits to know. A storyboard frame stands for a few seconds and can lag the moment it shows by about a second, so cut the real frame from a few candidate times around a hit (yb.download’s start/end). At 320x180 it finds scenes, not small props; a caption track can find what the picture cannot (search both).

Licence. A hit carries the package’s full rights record (illustration.schema.RIGHTS_FIELDS): the licence as the canonical code of illustration.licensing.normalize_license() (YouTube’s Creative Commons licence is by; its standard licence, which is all rights reserved, is youtube-standard-license; None when the sampler did not say), the licence URL, an attribution line, the author and their page, the source page (the video at that moment), and cacheable=False (the frames are never stored, only their embeddings). Fine to find and study; republishing follows the licence.

The corpus as one stacked float32 matrix, so a query is one matrix product.

search_frames() over a store reads and parses every video’s entry per query, so latency grows with the corpus. Build a FrameIndex once and pass it as index=: the queries then cost one (frames x dim) @ (dim,) product and the per-hit work only for the few that are kept. Rebuild it (FrameIndex.from_store again) after indexing more videos. Videos indexed with another model than model are left out and listed in other_model.

illustration.frames.index_video_frames(videos, *, store=None, sampler=<function storyboard_sampler>, scorer=None, model=None, refresh=False, batch=64)[source]#

Embed every frame of videos into the corpus; return what happened.

A video already in the store is skipped unless refresh=True (for a YouTube URL, without fetching anything), so indexing a channel again only does the new videos. A video that cannot be read or embedded (no storyboard, unavailable, a corrupt sheet, a scorer error) is recorded in failed with the reason, nothing is written for it, and the others go on. Frames are embedded batch at a time as they are read, so a long video never sits in memory whole.

Returns:

[ids], “skipped”: [ids], “failed”: {url: why}, “frames”: number of frames embedded}``; a video’s id is its video_id, else its URL.

Return type:

dict

illustration.frames.search_frames(text, *, store=None, scorer=None, model=None, k=10, per_video=3, min_gap_s=12.0, videos=None, index=None)[source]#

The k frames that best match text, best first.

Diversified, because the next frame of a match is usually a match too: at most per_video hits per video, and two hits closer than min_gap_s in one video are one scene (the better one is kept). videos limits the search to those video ids (or URLs, for a video that has no id). Videos indexed with another embedding model are skipped, with a warning. Pass index=FrameIndex.from_store(store) to search a prebuilt stacked matrix instead of reading the store (store is then not read).

Return type:

list[FrameHit]
