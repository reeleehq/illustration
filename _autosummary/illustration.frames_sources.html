illustration.frames_sources#

More ways into the frame corpus: frames from a local file, and a video’s captions (#59).

Both fit the seams illustration.frames already has, so nothing there changes.

  • file_sampler(): an every-N-seconds sampler for a local video, a sampler= for illustration.frames.index_video_frames(). Its identity is the file’s absolute path (the same keying as any id-less video since #56). Needs ffmpeg on the PATH and the [frames] extra’s pillow.

  • search_captions(): search a video’s subtitle cues beside its frames and get back the same FrameHit, whose t is the cue’s start. A caption finds what a 320x180 storyboard frame cannot (a held mug that is only mentioned). Scoring is lexical, on purpose: SigLIP embeds pictures and short captions, not a sentence of speech. The cue source is a seam (loader=), by default youtube_caption_loader() (yb).

    >>> parse_cues("WEBVTT\n\n00:00:01.000 --> 00:00:03.500\nhello <c>there</c>\n")
[(1.0, 'hello there')]

illustration.frames_sources.search_captions(text, videos, *, loader=<function youtube_caption_loader>, k=10, per_video=3, min_gap_s=12.0)[source]#

The k caption cues that best match text, best first, as FrameHit.

A cue scores the fraction of the query’s words it contains, plus 0.5 when it holds the whole phrase; cues with no query word are not hits. Diversified as illustration.frames.search_frames() is (per_video, min_gap_s). loader(url) returns (meta, cues): meta may carry video_id, title, license, author, author_url; a video it cannot read is skipped.

Return type:

list[FrameHit]
